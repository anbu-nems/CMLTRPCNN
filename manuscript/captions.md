# Figure Captions, PCNN ABO3 Manuscript

## Main Figure Legends


**Fig. 1 | Failure of classical theory and motivation for physics-constrained ML.**
**a**, Predicted versus measured permittivity for the Clausius–Mossotti (CM) baseline on the 1,124 *ABO*₃ compositions for which a valid CM evaluation is available, coloured by Reaney regime (Ia, Ib, II, III). The 1:1 line (black dashed) and ±10% band (grey shaded) mark the ideal prediction envelope; the CM relation underpredicts ε_r across the entire range and yields *R*² = −0.584 with mean absolute percentage error 45.9% (a negative *R*² indicates worse-than-mean prediction). **b**, Per-regime distribution of CM residuals (ε_r,measured − ε_r,CM) showing systematic underprediction largest in the tilted regimes II (median 25.0) and III (median 19.1), with the deviation increasing toward smaller tolerance factors. **c**, Histogram of the empirical enhancement ratio ε_r,measured / ε_r,CM across the 1,124 valid-CM compositions, with the no-enhancement reference (ratio = 1) marked. The distribution is centred well above unity (median 1.92), confirming that the soft-mode contribution typically carries the largest share of the dielectric response in the microwave regime and cannot be recovered by purely electronic theories. Together, these data motivate an architecture in which the soft-mode enhancement is explicitly modelled as an additive correction to a CM anchor.

**Fig. 2 | A-site soft-mode hierarchy discovered by the LST branch.**
**a**, Mean soft-mode amplification factor *f*_LST = δ_LST / ε_r,CM (the soft-mode contribution as a multiple of the electronic CM baseline) by A-site cation family, computed across the valid-CM compositions and stratified by primary A-site occupancy for the five dominant families (1,075 of the 1,124 valid-CM compositions; 49 minor-A-site compositions not shown): Pb (2.28×, *n* = 83), Ca (1.66×, *n* = 344), La (1.13×, *n* = 121), Sr (0.91×, *n* = 159), Ba (0.66×, *n* = 368). Error bars show bootstrap 95% confidence intervals from 2,000 resamples. The ordering Pb > Ca > La > Sr > Ba is statistically significant at *p* = 0.000 (*z* ≈ 14σ over 2,000 A-site label permutations). **b**, *f*_LST plotted against Shannon 12-coordinate ionic radius of the dominant A-site cation. The Ba→Sr→La→Ca segment follows a monotonic decrease consistent with cage-tightening enhancement of B-site off-centring; Pb deviates upward from this monotonic trend, consistent with 6*s*² lone-pair stereoactivity producing a quasi-electronic enhancement absent in alkaline-earth chemistry. **c**, Regime-resolved branch decomposition of ε_r (ε_r-weighted shares). Within each group the four branches sum to exactly 100% by construction: the model reconstructs ε_r as their exact additive sum (ε_r = ε_CM + δ_LST + δ_tilt + δ_res, reconstruction residual ≈ 0), and the residual branch is the exact complement δ_res ≡ ε_r − ε_CM − δ_LST − δ_tilt; displayed shares are rounded to 0.1%. Grouped horizontal bars on a logarithmic axis give the share contributed by the Clausius–Mossotti electronic baseline (ε_CM), soft-mode amplification (δ_LST), octahedral tilt (δ_tilt) and the residual (δ_res), for all valid-CM compositions (*n* = 1,124) and stratified by Reaney regime (Ia, *n* = 47; Ib, *n* = 302; II, *n* = 303; III, *n* = 472). δ_tilt is a negative (suppressive) contribution; its magnitude is drawn on the logarithmic axis (hatched bars) and the label retains its sign. The electronic baseline carries the bulk of ε_r in the low-tilt regimes (ε_CM = 72.9% in Ia, 65.6% in Ib), whereas soft-mode amplification becomes the majority contribution as octahedral tilting increases (δ_LST = 57.0% in II, 60.9% in III) — a crossover from electronic- to lattice-driven permittivity, with the overall corpus split (LST 54.1%, CM 46.2%, tilt −0.7%, residual 0.4%) shown in the top group.

**Fig. 3 | Physics-constrained neural network (PCNN) workflow.**
End-to-end pipeline from crystal chemistry to interpretable dielectric prediction, organised in four stages. **(1) Crystal-chemical space:** the curated 1,304-composition ABO₃ corpus spans A-site (Ba, Sr, Ca, La, Pb) and d⁰ B-site (Ti, Nb, Ta, Zr, Sn) chemistries across the Reaney tolerance-factor regimes (Ia–III), after filtering for perovskite validity and crystallographic consistency. **(2) Physics-guided descriptor representation:** each composition is encoded by physically grouped descriptors, structural (tolerance and octahedral factors, ionic-radius mismatch), lattice-dynamical (ω_TO/ω_LO surrogates, soft-mode and displacement proxies), bonding (electronegativity mismatch, covalency, bond-valence) and stability (global instability index), and embedded in a physics-informed representation space. **(3) Physics-constrained mechanism learning:** the network produces the additive decomposition ε_r = ε_CM + δ_LST + δ_tilt + δ_res, in which the Clausius–Mossotti baseline is parameter-free, the soft-mode branch is forced non-negative (Softplus), the octahedral-tilt branch is forced non-positive (−Softplus) and a confidence-gated residual captures remaining effects; hard sign constraints and physics regularisers (L_LST, L_GII, L_VCA) enforce physical consistency. **(4) Generalisation and reliability:** family-aware GroupShuffleSplit evaluation, a dual-layer applicability domain (kernel-density estimate + nearest-neighbour distance) and split-conformal calibration (90% nominal → 90.5% empirical coverage) establish where predictions are trustworthy. The pipeline yields the outcomes summarised at the foot of the figure: soft-mode amplification, octahedral-tilt suppression, the A-site lone-pair hierarchy (Pb > Ca > La > Sr > Ba), regime-resolved interpretation, calibrated uncertainty and Pb-free high-ε_r discovery.

**Fig. 4 | Branch decomposition and counterfactual analysis.**
**a**, Branch ablation: 3-fold Strat-GSS cross-validation *R*² (3 seeds, 500 epochs) when each branch is selectively removed and the model retrained, full PCNN (0.894), no CM (0.725, ΔR² = −0.169), no LST (0.206, ΔR² = −0.688), no tilt (0.895, ΔR² = +0.001), no residual (0.893, ΔR² = −0.001), and the CM anchor alone (−0.875). The LST branch is single-handedly responsible for the majority of model capacity; tilt and residual function as physics constraints rather than capacity providers. **b**, Counterfactual permutation analysis: for each B-site family, we compute the fraction of Δε_r assigned to the LST branch when the input is shuffled. The mean across families is 96.0% and the decomposition assigns ≥ 96% of Δε_r to δ_LST across every B-site family, demonstrating that the soft-mode channel is A-site-controlled and B-site-independent. **c**, Tilt-branch causal probe: zeroing the tilt branch output in Regime II + III holdout samples produces ΔR² = 0.0000, while removing the tilt branch entirely (panel a) is statistically indistinguishable. This indicates that the tilt branch operates through electronic coupling (top feature: χ_A, Pearson r = 0.531 with δ_tilt) rather than direct geometric mechanical suppression. **d**, Architectural regime priors versus observed *f*_LST per regime: the built-in tilt-damping priors (Ib = 1.5×, II = 0.9×, III = 0.7×) are overridden by A-site chemistry, Regime III (Ca-dominated) shows the highest observed *f*_LST = 1.516 despite the 0.7× damping prior, confirming that A-site identity outweighs tilt geometry in determining soft-mode amplification.

**Fig. 5 | Predictive performance, uncertainty calibration and OOD stability.**
**a**, Parity plot of PCNN predictions versus measured ε_r on the 116-sample family-disjoint holdout, coloured by Reaney regime (*R*² = 0.941, MAE = 5.77). Error bars represent the 90% conformal prediction interval (q̂₉₀ = 14.38 ε_r units). The 1:1 line (black) and ±10% band (grey) are shown for reference; 90.5% of holdout samples fall within the conformal band, matching the nominal 90% level. **b**, Calibration curve showing empirical coverage versus nominal coverage at 80%, 85%, 90% and 95% levels (filled circles), against the ideal diagonal (dashed line). Empirical coverage matches the nominal target to within 0.7 percentage points across all tested levels on this family-disjoint set (in-calibration consistency check; Methods). **c**, Comparison of cross-validation stability across seven baselines on the same Strat-GSS partition: PCNN (*R*² = 0.893 ± 0.043), CatBoost (0.930 ± 0.041), XGBoost (0.928 ± 0.053), RandomForest (0.901 ± 0.057), BPNN (0.914 ± 0.093), PIRNN (0.863 ± 0.181), Ridge (0.721 ± 0.190). The PCNN exhibits the lowest variance among neural-network baselines (4× lower than PIRNN, 2× lower than BPNN), reflecting the regularising effect of architectural sign constraints. **d**, Train→test *R*² gap for each baseline; PCNN (0.047) is 3.6× smaller than XGBoost (0.169) and 2.5× smaller than RF (0.117), consistent with reduced overfitting in OOD partitions.

**Fig. 6 | Extrapolation boundaries, uncertainty-aware screening and inverse design.**
**a**, Leave-one-family-out (LOFO) generalisation: holdout *R*² when each A-site family is removed entirely from training and used as test (Pb −0.097, Ba 0.008, Sr 0.434, Ca 0.610, La 0.849). The progression reflects increasing chemical isolation; Pb is the only family on which the PCNN fundamentally fails to extrapolate, identifying 6*s*² lone-pair stereoactivity as a hard ML boundary (regime-LOFO is shown in Supplementary Fig. 8). **b**, The confident-but-wrong failure mode: per-A-site fraction of compositions classified as in-domain by the conjunctive σ_conf × KDE applicability-domain gate (bars; Pb 74.4%, La 72.0%, Ba 58.3%, Ca 52.7%, Sr 23.3%, ordered by increasing mean σ_conf) overlaid with the corresponding LOFO *R*² (markers, right axis). Pb is paradoxically the most "confident" family (lowest σ_conf = 0.288, highest in-AD) yet has the worst LOFO performance (*R*² = −0.097), demonstrating that uncertainty magnitude alone cannot detect novel-chemistry failure modes; Sr (in-AD = 23.3%) is correctly flagged as uncertain. **c**, Uncertainty-aware virtual screening of 72 Pb-free candidate compositions: predicted ε_r with 90% conformal interval (point + whiskers), with the inclusion thresholds (predicted ε_r ≥ 80, green dashed; 90% lower bound ≥ 65, orange dotted) marked. 70 of 72 candidates pass the conjunctive filter (97% hit rate); the top candidates (Sr₀.₉Ca₀.₁TiO₃, Sr₀.₉₅Ca₀.₀₅TiO₃, Sr₀.₅Ca₀.₅TiO₃) are all Sr-Ca-Ti solid solutions, consistent with the A-site hierarchy in Fig. 2a. **d**, Physics-constrained genetic-algorithm (GA) inverse design: best predicted ε_r per generation for three runs (Pb-allowed, *d*⁰-strict, Pb-free; ≈ 5,000 PCNN evaluations each). All runs exceed the curated-screen optimum (140, grey dashed). The Pb-free optimum Ba₀.₀₅Sr₀.₉₅Ti₀.₈₄Zr₀.₁₅O₃ reaches predicted ε_r ≈ 147 (90% LCB 132) and is δ_LST-dominated (*f*_LST = 2.85), but its nearest-neighbour distance to the training set exceeds the 95th percentile, flagging it as a confidence-bounded compositional extrapolation (synthesis target) rather than a validated material.

**Supplementary Fig. 8 | Leave-one-family-out (LOFO) generalisability, detailed.** **a**, A-site leave-one-family-out (LOFO) holdout *R*²: La 0.849 (*n* = 125), Ca 0.610 (*n* = 421), Sr 0.434 (*n* = 180), Ba 0.008 (*n* = 436), Pb −0.097 (*n* = 86). **b**, Regime LOFO holdout *R*²: II 0.654 (*n* = 331), III 0.490 (*n* = 559), Ib −0.158 (*n* = 354), Ia −7.378 (*n* = 60). **Supplementary Fig. 14 | Permutation null distributions.** Null distribution of the family-mean *f*_LST range under 2,000 random permutations of the grouping labels. **a**, A-site permutation null: the observed range (1.622; Pb 2.28 → Ba 0.66) lies far in the tail (*z* ≈ 17σ, *p* < 0.0005; 0 of 2,000 permutations as extreme). **b**, Regime permutation null: observed range 1.136 (*z* ≈ 9σ, *p* < 0.0005). **Supplementary Fig. 20 | The three physics mechanisms encoded in the PCNN.** Schematic of the ABO₃ perovskite responses the model decomposes: (**a**) the ideal cubic lattice setting the Clausius–Mossotti baseline ε_r,CM (polarizability of the static lattice); (**b**) soft-mode (B-site off-centering) displacement giving the Lyddane–Sachs–Teller enhancement δ_LST ∝ 1/ω²_TO; and (**c**) octahedral tilting (Goldschmidt/Reaney, tolerance factor *t* < 1) that suppresses ε_r (δ_tilt). A-site, B-site and oxygen sites are indicated in the legend.

**Supplementary Fig. 21 | Structure-property correlations between 12 physical descriptors and PCNN-predicted ε_r.** Each panel (**a**–**l**) shows the predicted ε_r against one descriptor across all 1,304 ABO₃-type compositions, with points coloured by Reaney regime (Ia, Ib, II, III). The annotated Spearman rank correlation *ρ* quantifies the monotonic relation between each descriptor and the predicted permittivity. Panels: **a**, Goldschmidt tolerance factor *t* (*ρ* = −0.04). **b**, Clausius–Mossotti baseline ε_r,CM (*ρ* = +0.41). **c**, soft-mode branch δ_LST (*ρ* = +0.87, the strongest single predictor, consistent with the LST-dominated mechanism reported in the main text). **d**, octahedral tilt branch δ_tilt (*ρ* = +0.48). **e**, residual branch δ_res (*ρ* = +0.61). **f**, predictive confidence σ_conf (*ρ* = −0.06). **g**, soft-mode enhancement factor *f*_LST = δ_LST / ε_r,CM (*ρ* = +0.76). **h**, B-site d⁰ fraction (*ρ* = +0.61). **i**, total ionic polarizability α_total (*ρ* = −0.02). **j**, A-site electronegativity χ_A (*ρ* = +0.41). **k**, global instability index GII (*ρ* = −0.15). **l**, ensemble standard deviation σ_ML (*ρ* = +0.48). The ranking δ_LST > *f*_LST > δ_res ≈ d⁰_per_field > χ_A confirms that the soft-mode channel carries the largest single contribution to permittivity, in agreement with the SHAP attribution in Supplementary Fig. 11 and the branch-decomposition statistics in the main text.
